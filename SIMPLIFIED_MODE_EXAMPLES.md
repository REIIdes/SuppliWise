# Simplified Mode - Content Examples

## Goal
Make recommendations so simple that **anyone** can understand them, regardless of education level or medical knowledge.

---

## Writing Guidelines

### ✅ DO:
- Use everyday words (5th grade reading level)
- Keep sentences short (10-15 words max)
- Focus on benefits, not mechanisms
- Be direct and friendly
- Sound like talking to a friend

### ❌ DON'T:
- Use medical terminology
- Mention conditions or symptoms
- Explain "how" or "why" scientifically
- Reference the assessment ("Based on your...")
- Use phrases like "may support", "mechanism", "synthesis"

---

## Summary Examples

### ❌ BAD (Too Technical)
> "Based on your limited sun exposure and reported fatigue symptoms, this personalized supplement plan addresses potential vitamin D and B12 deficiencies while supporting cellular energy metabolism and neurotransmitter function."

### ✅ GOOD (Simple & Direct)
> "This plan can help you feel more energized and sleep better."

### More Good Examples:
- "These supplements may help reduce your tiredness and improve your focus."
- "This routine is designed to boost your energy and support your wellness."
- "Your plan focuses on helping you feel better every day."

## Recommendation Reason Examples

### Vitamin D

**❌ BAD (References conditions)**
> "Based on your limited sun exposure and indoor lifestyle, Vitamin D3 can help support immune function and mood regulation by facilitating calcium absorption and serotonin production."

**✅ GOOD (Simple benefit)**
> "Helps boost your immune system and improve mood."

### Magnesium

**❌ BAD (Too technical)**
> "Because you reported moderate fatigue and sleep issues, Magnesium Glycinate may help by supporting ATP synthesis, reducing neuromuscular excitability, and regulating circadian rhythm."

**✅ GOOD (Simple benefit)**
> "Helps you relax, sleep better, and feel less tired."

### Omega-3

**❌ BAD (Medical jargon)**
> "Given your inflammatory markers and cardiovascular risk profile, Omega-3 fatty acids may reduce systemic inflammation and support endothelial function through EPA and DHA mechanisms."

**✅ GOOD (Simple benefit)**
> "Supports heart health and reduces inflammation."

### Vitamin B12

**❌ BAD (References symptoms)**
> "Since you reported cognitive fog and fatigue, B12 (Methylcobalamin) supports energy production via methylation pathways and red blood cell formation."

**✅ GOOD (Simple benefit)**
> "Boosts your energy and helps you think more clearly."

### Probiotics

**❌ BAD (Too detailed)**
> "For your digestive symptoms and immune concerns, probiotics restore gut microbiome balance, enhance barrier function, and modulate immune response."

**✅ GOOD (Simple benefit)**
> "Supports healthy digestion and immune system."

### Ashwagandha

**❌ BAD (References assessment)**
> "Based on your reported stress levels and cortisol dysregulation, Ashwagandha acts as an adaptogen to normalize HPA axis function."

**✅ GOOD (Simple benefit)**
> "Helps reduce stress and promote relaxation."

## Evidence Examples

### ❌ BAD (Technical citations)
> "Holick et al. (2011) Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism established Vitamin D deficiency guidelines (PMID: 21646368); NIH Office of Dietary Supplements Vitamin D Fact Sheet confirms efficacy."

### More Good Examples:

- "Studies confirm this is effective."
- "Scientifically backed and safe."
- "Widely researched and trusted."

## Complete Recommendation Comparison

### Vitamin D Example

#### DETAILED MODE (Medical)
```
Name: Vitamin D3 (Cholecalciferol)

Reason: Because you reported seasonal mood changes and limited 
sun exposure (<15 minutes daily), Vitamin D3 may help by supporting 
immune function through T-cell modulation and serotonin synthesis 
via tryptophan hydroxylase activation in the brain.

Recommended for: Limited Sun Exposure, Seasonal Mood Changes, 
Immune Support

Context: Based on your indoor work environment and reported 
winter blues, optimizing Vitamin D levels is a foundational step.

Evidence: Holick et al. (2011) Journal of Clinical Endocrinology 
& Metabolism - Vitamin D deficiency guidelines (PMID: 21646368); 
NIH Office of Dietary Supplements Vitamin D Fact Sheet.
```

#### SIMPLIFIED MODE (Easy)
```
Name: Vitamin D3 (Cholecalciferol)

Reason: Helps boost your immune system and improve mood.

[No "Recommended for" shown]
[No "Context" shown]

Evidence: Research shows this helps most people.
```

## Magnesium Example

#### DETAILED MODE
```
Name: Magnesium Glycinate

Reason: Because you reported moderate fatigue and difficulty 
falling asleep, Magnesium Glycinate may help by supporting ATP 
synthesis for cellular energy, reducing neuromuscular excitability, 
and regulating GABA neurotransmission for better sleep quality.

Recommended for: Fatigue (Moderate), Sleep Issues, Muscle Tension

Context: Based on your stress levels and exercise routine, 
magnesium is often depleted and needs replenishment.

Evidence: Nielsen et al. (2016) Nutrients - magnesium deficiency 
linked to fatigue and insomnia (PMID: 27187450); Abbasi et al. 
(2012) Journal of Research in Medical Sciences - magnesium 
supplementation improves sleep (PMID: 23853635).
```

#### SIMPLIFIED MODE
```
Name: Magnesium Glycinate

Reason: Helps you relax, sleep better, and feel less tired.

[No "Recommended for" shown]
```

## Tone Comparison

| Aspect | Detailed Mode | Simplified Mode |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Audience** | Healthcare professionals | General public |
| **Reading Level** | College/Medical | 5th grade |
| **Sentence Length** | 20-30 words | 10-15 words |
| **Terminology** | Medical terms | Everyday words |
| **Focus** | Mechanisms | Benefits |
| **Citations** | Full references | Simple reassurance |
| **Conditions Mentioned** | Yes, explicitly | No, never |

---

## Word Choice Guide

### Replace Technical Terms

| ❌ Don't Use | ✅ Use Instead |
|-------------|---------------|
| "synthesis" | "production" or omit |
| "modulation" | "support" or omit |
| "facilitate" | "help" |
| "optimize" | "improve" |
| "deficiency" | "low levels" or omit |
| "metabolism" | "energy" |
| "cognitive function" | "focus" or "thinking" |
| "neurotransmitter" | omit (just say benefit) |
| "inflammation" | "swelling" or just say benefit |
| "immune function" | "immune system" |
| "circadian rhythm" | "sleep schedule" |
| "ATP production" | "energy" or omit |

## AI Prompt Tips

When training the AI, emphasize:

1. **Maximum 15 words** for simplifiedReason
2. **No "Based on..."** phrases
3. **No symptom/condition mentions**
4. **Focus only on the benefit**
5. **Sound like a friend, not a doctor**

---

## Quality Checklist

Before releasing simplified content, verify:

- [ ] Can a 5th grader understand it?
- [ ] No medical words used?
- [ ] Under 15 words for reasons?
- [ ] No conditions/symptoms mentioned?
- [ ] No "Based on your..." phrases?
- [ ] Sounds friendly and encouraging?
- [ ] Benefits are clear?
- [ ] Would you send this to your non-medical friend?

---

## Testing with Real Users

Ask someone with no medical background:
1. "What does this supplement do?"
2. "Why should you take it?"
3. "Is this confusing in any way?"

If they hesitate or ask for clarification, it's **too complex**.

---

**Goal:** Make it so simple that your grandmother, a teenager, and someone with limited English can all understand it immediately.

**Test:** If you need to explain what a word means, it's too technical.

**Success:** User reads it and immediately knows "this helps me sleep better" or "this gives me more energy" without any confusion.
